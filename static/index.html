One engine, four stages

The model reads. The law decides. Those are deliberately separate jobs — an extractor that never judges legality, and a rules engine that never guesses.

  1. 1

    Extract

    A language model parses each document into a fixed schema — deposit amount, months' rent, licence status, what you were handed at signing. It never decides whether anything is legal.

  2. 2

    Adjudicate

    A deterministic rules engine joins those facts against a statute table of verified Philadelphia and Pennsylvania rules. Every flag carries the section that produced it.

  3. 3

    Track

    Events that start statutory clocks become dated deadlines — like the 30 days a landlord has to return a deposit once you've given a written forwarding address.

  4. 4

    Answer

    A grounded agent answers questions using only your vault and the statute table. When it can't ground an answer, it says so and hands you to a human.

What it does

Everything listed here is built and working.

Upload a whole lease at once

A real Philadelphia lease is 25 PDFs, not one. Select the entire bundle: it files as a single timeline entry, and the documents that actually carry your terms are the ones read.

Findings with citations

Each finding names the statute section behind it. A claim without a source is treated as a bug, not a style choice.

Checked, not just flagged

A clean lease is a result. You see what passed, what failed, and what can't be judged yet — with the next step for each unknown.

Deadlines that start themselves

Record a move-out with a written forwarding address and the 30-day deposit clock appears, dated, before you'd have thought to ask.

An agent that refuses

Ask questions of your own record. Answers cite a vault entry or a statute row — and when neither supports an answer, it declines and routes you to a human.

Tenant view and landlord view

One rule, two framings. The 30-day deposit window is a tenant's right and a landlord's obligation — the same row, read from your side.

A read-only link for the other side

Share the record without handing over control. The link can't write to your vault, and one click timestamps that the other party saw it.

A printable evidence packet

One chronological page — timeline, findings, citations, deadlines — that prints straight to PDF for a hearing or a mediator.

Right to Counsel check

Covered zip codes route to the Philly Tenant Hotline. Everyone else gets PhillyTenant.org, because most renters are outside the program.

The agent is a query interface, not an oracle

Its retrieval surface is your vault and the statute table — nothing else. Every answer must cite one of them, and the citation is verified against your actual records before you ever see it. A claimed source that doesn't exist becomes a refusal, no matter how confident the model was.

The refusal isn't a limitation we're apologising for. It's the feature.

      What it does not do

      Stated plainly, because a tool in this space that overclaims is worse than none.

      • It is not legal advice and creates no lawyer–client relationship. It tells you what the law says and where to read it.
      • Philadelphia and Pennsylvania only. The engine is jurisdiction-agnostic, but the only statute table that ships is this one.
      • It will not answer from general knowledge. If your vault and the table don't support an answer, you get a refusal and a phone number.
      • Unverified rules stay hidden. Rules without a confirmed pin cite are marked draft and never render, even when they'd match.
      • It cannot check a rental licence for you. That lives in L&I's records, not your lease — the vault tells you to go and look.
